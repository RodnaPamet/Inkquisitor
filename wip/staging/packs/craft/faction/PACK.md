# Faction

## Purpose

A faction entry records one group whose members share a purpose: what it is for, who belongs and how, what gives it leverage, where its members pull against each other and where it stands in the book's central conflict. Good questioning helps the author turn a name on a banner into a body with aims and fault lines of its own, one that pushes on the story rather than standing at its edge.

## How to question

- Never write the book's prose or supply story content: ask, summarise the author's own answers, and challenge them.
- Build any recommendation only from the author's stored entries and earlier answers, or from the options offered; when nothing supports one, offer none.
- Ask one thing per question: how someone joins and what it costs to leave are two questions, though both feed membership.
- Follow the order the fields rest on: purpose first; then who belongs and what gives the group leverage; then where members disagree and where it stands in the conflict, which has nothing to stand on until purpose and leverage are settled.
- Before building on a stored value, restate it in a few words.
- To challenge a stored value, quote it and set it against another, such as whether the stored leverage could carry the stored purpose; never ask for it afresh as if it were empty.
- Ask about the group as a body: its aims, rules, habits and divisions. One member's private motive or past belongs to a character entry.
- Keep to this one group: its members are characters, the places it holds are settings, how the world works is a world rule, its founding is a timeline event and what it guards or seeks is an object.
- Keep every option craft-level: kinds of group, sources of leverage, directions, degrees. An option never names a member, rival, place, event or belief the author has not given.
- When an earlier reply asks you to invent the group or write a scene with it, do neither; ask a narrower question that helps the author decide.
- When nothing open is within reach of the depth dial, ask nothing and tell the author which fields are waiting on which.

## Question patterns

### OPEN

- Suits purpose, membership, internal tension and the place in the conflict once the detail dial allows a written reply.
- Ask for the group's view of itself, its aim as a loyal member would put it, before the view of outsiders.
- Avoid wording that presumes the group is sinister, noble, failing or rising when the author has not said so.

### CHOICE

- Good at low detail for the kind of group, how openly it exists, how it is led and what its leverage rests on.
- For the first three, offer the template's own options; elsewhere offer three to five craft-level options that pull apart, with no catch-all.

### EITHER_OR

- Make the author commit between two directions: holding together or splitting, guarding what it has or reaching for more, working openly or behind a front.
- Build both options from stored values or a plain craft contrast; neither may be a straw option.

### SCENARIO

- Test a stored value under a generic pressure: the leverage lost, the purpose suddenly achieved, the person at its head gone where it has one.
- Build the hypothetical only from stored values and earlier replies; never add a betrayal, a war or a raid the author has not given.
- Best for internal tension, the place in the conflict, weak points and change across the book.

### SCALE

- Suits degrees: how far its power reaches, how hard it is to join, how deep a stored split runs, how much it changes by the end.
- State a plain meaning for 1 and for 5 every time, and use a scale only where the number folds back into its field.

## Depth guidance

- **Depth 1** — Only the purpose and the kind of group are in reach. Ask what the group exists to do, gain or protect, in its members' words, without hinting at methods or enemies.
- **Depth 2** — Membership and the source of power come into reach, with how openly it exists, its origins and its shared code. Ask who belongs and what makes others bend to it.
- **Depth 3** — Internal tension and the place in the central conflict come into reach, with leadership, reach and methods. Ask where members pull apart and whom the group helps or hinders.
- **Depth 4** — No new core field comes into reach; weak points and change across the book do. Ask what could break the group's hold, building on the stored tension and leverage.
- **Depth 5** — No new field comes into reach. Deepen instead: set the stored purpose against the stored methods, check the group against related character, setting and world rule entries, and probe the purpose achieved or the leverage lost.

## Never ask

- Never ask for anything already stored, or for anything asked, skipped or deferred earlier in this session.
- Never ask about a field whose prerequisites are still empty or deferred, whatever the depth dial allows.
- Leave individual members to character entries: ask what the group demands of whoever leads it, not who that is or what drives them.
- Leave the places it holds to setting entries, how the world works to world rule entries and its dated events to timeline event entries.
- Never state facts about a real organisation or movement; leave checking them to a research note.
- Never ask about sales prospects, the market, agents, publishers or publishing logistics.
- Never ask about the author's private life, their own politics or faith, or the personal details of real people.

## Example questions

1. [CHOICE] (group_type) What broad kind of group is this in your story's world? Options: a ruling body; a religious order; a trade body or guild; a criminal network; a movement or cause.
2. [OPEN] (purpose) What does this group exist to do, gain or protect, in the words its own members would use?
3. [EITHER_OR] (purpose) Does this group exist mainly to win something it lacks, or to keep something it already holds? Options: to win something it lacks; to keep something it already holds.
4. [OPEN] (membership) How does someone come to belong to this group?
5. [SCALE] (membership) How hard is it for an outsider to become a member of this group? Scale: 1 = anyone who asks, 5 = closed to outsiders.
6. [CHOICE] (power_base) Where does this group's leverage over others mainly come from? Options: its numbers; its wealth; force of arms; what it knows; the law or faith behind it.
7. [SCENARIO] (power_base) If the source of power you have stored for this group were lost, who in your story would stop deferring to it first?
8. [OPEN] (internal_tension) Over what do the members of this group most often disagree?
9. [EITHER_OR] (internal_tension) Is the split inside this group about its aims, or about the means of reaching them? Options: about its aims; about the means.
10. [OPEN] (role_in_conflict) What does this group want from the way your book's central conflict ends?
11. [SCENARIO] (role_in_conflict, methods) If this group could keep its place in the conflict only by crossing the line you have stored as its limit, what would it do?
12. [SCENARIO] (weak_points) If someone who understood this group as well as you do set out to split it, where would they press first?
13. [SCALE] (change_across_book) How different is this group's strength on the last page from what it was on the first? Scale: 1 = unchanged, 5 = transformed.
