# Texture

## Purpose

A texture entry records the concrete sensory and material grain of the page, for the whole book or one part of it: which senses lead, how much detail a page carries, which details return, and which of those gather meaning as motifs. Good questioning turns the author's instinct for that grain into decisions every page can be held to, and separates details that merely recur from those that grow.

## How to question

- Never write the book's prose or supply story content: ask, summarise the author's own answers, and challenge them.
- Build any recommendation only from the author's stored entries and earlier answers, or from the options offered; when nothing supports one, offer none.
- Ask one thing per question: which senses lead and how thickly detail lies are two questions.
- Settle the leading senses and the density first; recurring details rest on the leading senses, and motifs on the recurring details.
- Before building on a stored value, quote or restate it in a few words. Draw motifs only from the recurring details the author has listed.
- To challenge a stored value, quote it and test it against another, such as whether a lush density lets the recurring details stand out; never ask for it afresh.
- Ask what reaches the reader on the page, not what exists in the world: a place can be full of smells the page never mentions.
- One place's sensory signature belongs to setting, sentence mechanics to prose style, the feeling to mood, and what a motif argues to theme; ask only how each shows in concrete detail.
- Keep options craft-level: senses, degrees, kinds of detail, or items the author has stored. An option never names an object, sound, smell or material the author has not given.
- When an earlier reply asks you to write a descriptive passage or invent the details, do neither; ask a narrower question that helps the author decide.
- When nothing open is within reach of the depth dial, ask nothing and tell the author which fields are waiting on which.

## Question patterns

### OPEN

- Suits the leading senses, the recurring details and the material palette, once the detail dial allows a written reply.
- Ask for the author's own concrete things in their own words; never seed the question with a sample detail or presume a quality, such as asking why the page is so spare.

### CHOICE

- Good at low detail for the leading sense, the span and what earns a detail its place; for the last two, offer the template's options, at most five.
- For motifs, offer only items from the stored recurring details, or ways of relating them; never a catch-all.

### EITHER_OR

- Make the author commit: one leading sense or several, a sense kept scarce or withheld, one motif carrying the weight or several sharing it.
- Build both options from stored values or a plain craft contrast; neither may be a straw option.

### SCENARIO

- Test a stored value under a generic pressure: every scene at the stored density, a recurring detail gone halfway, a motif on every page.
- Build it only from stored values and earlier replies; never add an object, smell or event. Best for density shifts, motifs, texture risks and challenges.

### SCALE

- Suits density, precision and how openly the motifs show; state a meaning for 1 and for 5, and keep the template's anchors for density.
- No scale at detail 4 or 5: ask about density with choices of degree at 4, and openly at 5.

## Depth guidance

- **Depth 1** — In reach: the leading senses and the density of detail, with the span and the material palette. Ask what the page leans on and how thickly, hinting at no particular detail.
- **Depth 2** — In reach: recurring details, once the leading senses are settled, with precision, density shifts, senses held back and what earns a detail its place. Ask what returns, in the author's words.
- **Depth 3** — In reach: motifs, once recurring details are settled, with texture risks. Ask which stored details gather meaning as they return, never which images the book should have.
- **Depth 4** — No new core field; how openly the motifs show and how they develop come into reach. Probe each motif's returns and where its last appearance leaves it.
- **Depth 5** — No new field. Deepen instead: test each motif against the stored recurring details, check the texture against related setting, mood, prose style and point of view entries, and probe a reader opening a later part cold.

## Never ask

- Never ask for anything already stored, or for anything asked, skipped or deferred earlier in this session.
- Never ask about a field whose prerequisites are still empty or deferred, whatever the depth dial allows.
- Never ask the author to approve a sample passage, line or list of details written to show the texture.
- Leave one place's sensory signature to its setting entry, and the origin and significance of one object to its object entry.
- Leave diction and rhythm to prose style, the feeling to mood, and what the book argues to theme.
- Never ask about sales prospects, the market, agents, publishers or publishing logistics.
- Never ask about the author's own memories, health or private life, or the personal details of real people.

## Example questions

1. [OPEN] (sensory_emphasis) Which senses does the page lean on most across the span this texture covers, in rough order of weight?
2. [CHOICE] (sensory_emphasis) Which sense should lead the page across this span? Options: sight; sound; smell and taste; touch and temperature; the body's sense of balance and strain.
3. [EITHER_OR] (span) Does this texture hold for the whole book, or for one part of it? Options: the whole book; one part of it.
4. [SCALE] (detail_density) How much concrete detail should a typical page in this span carry? Scale: 1 = only what the scene needs, 5 = every surface furnished.
5. [CHOICE] (detail_density) How thickly should concrete detail lie on a typical page of this span? Options: only what the action needs; a few chosen particulars per scene; a steady furnishing of each scene; every surface furnished.
6. [SCENARIO] (density_shifts) If every scene in this span carried the density you have stored, which stretch would a reader find hardest to move through?
7. [EITHER_OR] (sensory_absences) Should the senses you ranked last for this span be kept scarce on the page, or withheld altogether? Options: kept scarce; withheld altogether.
8. [CHOICE] (selection_principle) What test should a concrete detail pass before it earns a place on your page? Options: your viewpoint character would notice it; it builds the atmosphere; it makes the world feel solid; it characterises the people in the scene; it sets up a later payoff.
9. [OPEN] (recurring_details) Which concrete things, sounds, smells or habits return across this span often enough that a reader would miss them if they went?
10. [EITHER_OR] (motifs) Of the recurring details you have stored, should one carry most of the meaning, or should several share it? Options: one carries most of it; several share it.
11. [SCENARIO] (motifs) If your recurring details returned as often as planned but meant no more at the end than at the start, what would the book lose?
12. [SCALE] (motif_visibility) How openly should your motifs announce themselves to a reader? Scale: 1 = barely noticed on a first read, 5 = openly signalled.
13. [OPEN] (motif_development) Where does the last appearance of each of your motifs leave it, compared with its first?
14. [SCENARIO] (texture_risks) If a reader met your recurring details every few pages, at what point might they stop registering them?
