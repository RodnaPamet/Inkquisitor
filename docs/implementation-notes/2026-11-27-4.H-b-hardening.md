## Decisions

- **The rules were below the bar, and were fixed narrowly.** Over the new set
  the rules raised 11 false alarms in 33 (precision 0.67). Each fix refuses one
  class and leaves the pair to the deep check: a NEGATED statement states no
  value ("Marrow is not a miller" vs "a smith"); two values are compared only
  when both are NAMES or both DESCRIPTIONS (a name's first word written with a
  capital after an article) — "Paris" and "the capital of France" may be one
  place; values are compared with accents folded ("Zürich", "Zurich"); an AGE
  relative to someone ("three years younger than Tomas") is not an age; a
  KNOWLEDGE proposition keeps its question word (knowing THAT the deed was
  forged is not knowing WHO forged it); and two facts placed in DIFFERENT orders
  (a beat's and a timeline event's) do not overlap by default — each is at a
  known point the clock cannot compare, which is not "unknown" (one 4.2 unit
  assertion changed with it, its comment saying why). Recall stayed 1.0.
- **One false alarm stays, counted.** Two synonyms of one shape ("a physician",
  "a doctor") are raised: no rule can tell them apart without a thesaurus, and a
  thesaurus is not narrow. The set keeps the case so the residual is visible.
- **The deep check's per-case strictness is unchanged.** A near miss raised
  still fails its case (4.2's grader); the set gains four near-miss cases (the
  roadmap's classes, one seeded pair among them), and every check now reports
  the false-alarm rate, the false alarms by case and the recall beside the
  precision. Production keeps D11 (no model record is committed).
- **The backfill writes outbox rows, never a second extraction path.** An entry
  with at least one version and no extraction row at all was last saved before
  4.1 (the outbox trigger writes one for every version since). The backfill
  gives its NEWEST version a row of origin BACKFILL and queues it as a
  `bible-facts` job, so every budget, D11, the rate minute, the kill switch,
  coalescing (a later save supersedes it) and the resolve are the existing
  ones. The minutely dispatcher's due window asks `origin = 'SAVE'`; a backfill
  row is the hourly job's alone, so a backlog of old entries never takes a
  minute's places from a save.
- **The daily share is measured as the nightly share is.** A sub-purpose of the
  bible-facts prompt (`facts.bible-extract.backfill`, `callPurposeOf` refuses
  any purpose that is not the template's or a sub-purpose of it): the same
  prompt, record and gate, its spend apart in `AiCallLog` and the reservations.
  The window is the last 24 hours, rolling. The job asks the share (and the
  ceiling) before queuing a workspace; the extraction asks it before each call
  with that call's worst case; the ledger asks it again under its spend lock.
  A stop at the share is DAILY_SHARE, resumable, and spends no attempt.
- **Hourly, at :37, and bounded a tick.** At most 20 entries of a workspace and
  200 rows across workspaces a tick, so a large bible is read over hours or
  days at the share, never in a burst; no other schedule uses minute 37.
- **A backfill row runs as the workspace's oldest OWNER when its author may no
  longer edit.** A version saved before 4.1 was often written by someone gone;
  skipping it as AUTHOR_NOT_PERMITTED would leave the backfill unable to read
  those entries at all. The backfill is the product catching up, as the nightly
  continuity check is, and acts as that check does. A SAVE row still runs only
  as its version's author.
- **Discovery reads distinct workspaces from a round-robin cursor.** The
  minutely discovery took workspaces by their oldest due row, so 200
  workspaces with old backlogs held every tick's places. It now takes them in
  id order after the last one served (`JobCursor`, global: a job name and a
  workspace id, reached only through the discovery's bypass, no app_user
  grant), wrapping round: every busy workspace within ⌈busy ÷ 200⌉ ticks. The
  backfill shares the helper (`fromCursor`); the purge applies the 4.H-a
  distinct-workspace pattern (an even share each) instead, since it runs once a
  day and a cursor buys nothing there.
- **Passed-over subjects are retried weekly when nothing else changes.** The
  next pass retries them (D32), but a book is queued only when it changes; a
  book with only outstanding subjects is due again a week after its last run
  (`CONTINUITY_PASSED_OVER_RETRY_MS`), so a subject that fails every night is
  not paid for every night. A remembered subject with no live fact left is
  dropped (unless the fact read was cut). Past 500 remembered subjects the next
  pass checks every subject (`passedOverCut`), cleared at a pass's completion
  unless the list is still full.
- **The guard's version is a hash pin, not a convention.** The test hashes the
  five files that decide a refusal (rules, normalisation, both scanners, the
  enforcement table); any edit there fails it until `GUARD_RULES_VERSION` moves
  and the new hash is recorded beside it. A comment edit asks for a bump too: a
  false bump costs one re-send of each refused section, a missed one never
  re-sends anything.
- **A refused section is re-sent in calls of its own.** Sections are packed into
  calls together, so a refusal records every section of its call; a retry sent
  with the version's changed sections would let a second refusal take their
  facts down with it. Retries are planned separately, after the changed
  sections. Refusals recorded before this change (4.H-a's DONE GUARD_REFUSED rows)
  name no sections and are never retried.
- **A standing stale flag is not rewritten.** The raise's `ON CONFLICT … DO
  UPDATE` now has a `WHERE`: a flag that is OPEN and still standing already says
  what the save would say, so no tuple is written and no guard trigger fires;
  a dismissed, cleared or cleared-by-save flag is raised again with this change
  as its base. The readers take the change's END from the upstream element as it
  stands (its newest content version), not from the flag's
  `upstreamVersionId`, which now names the save that opened the debt.
- **The sticky-note flake was a race with tldraw's own caret placement.**
  tldraw's `RichTextArea` places the caret after its TipTap editor mounts — at
  the clicked point in `onCreate` (a `setTimeout(0)`), and again on a 100 ms
  timer — and Playwright's per-key typing lands inside those frames, so a key
  could be inserted at the clicked point before the earlier ones. The spec
  waits for focus, inserts the text as ONE input event (`insertText`), and
  presses Escape only once the note holds exactly that text. A person's first
  key follows a click by far more than 100 ms; no product change.
