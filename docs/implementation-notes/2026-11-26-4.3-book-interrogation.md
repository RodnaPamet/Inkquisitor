# 2026-11-26 — 4.3 Book-level interrogation (D29)

**Commit:** uncommitted (branch `wip/4.3`, cut from `main` at `4094e25`)

Until 4.3 every interview questioned one element, a selection, or a slot. 4.3
adds three ways of questioning the WHOLE book, all on one Interrogate page
(`/w/<slug>/books/<id>/interrogate`, linked from the book board): the stress
test (a deep pass that finds weak points and turns each into questions), the
gap hunt (which empty fields hold up the most of the book, and three sessions
to fill them) and the persona panel (the book questioned by a Target Reader, a
Ruthless Editor and a Genre Specialist, questions grouped by who asked). It also
adds compaction for long interviews, built and evaluated but off until 4.4.
Nothing in 4.3 writes the bible: every path ends in questions.

## Design

### The stress test

The input is one character budget (`STRESS_INPUT_CHAR_BUDGET`, 40,000) split
in shares across the book, its elements, the story order, top facts, open
threads and passages (`buildStressInput`); every element, fact, thread and
passage shown is given a tag (`E3`, `F7`, `T2`, `P1`), and the call's `shown`
map is what citations are checked against. What did not fit is counted per
category (`StressCoverage`, shown beside the estimate and on each run). The
worst case is 2.1's formula over the exact blocks the call will send, plus one
repair; `fitStressInput` halves the budget until the worst case is inside the
template's per-call budget, else refuses `CALL_OVER_BUDGET` before anything is
sent. The author confirms the "at most", which becomes the job's ceiling.

A weak point is a kind, a short label and one to three questions, citing at
least one tag. The validator's order is in `validate.ts`'s header; the two
that matter most for D3 are REWRITES_TEXT (a quoted run longer than five words
the book does not hold is replacement wording) and INVENTS_NAME (a capitalised
word past a sentence's first that the book does not hold). INVENTS_NAME came
out of the injection suite: a compromised model naming the other book's canary
character passed every other rule.

### The gap hunt

Candidates are computed in code (`candidates.ts`): for each kind present and
each field its template declares, `weight = 10 + 10·dependents + 5·core +
min(10, relations) + min(10, facts) + 2·min(10, citations)` (dependents counted
transitively through `dependsOn`; the last three from the kind's rows), and
`score = weight × emptyElements / elements`. The top twelve go to the model,
numbered, as product words and counts. The model may only reorder them and
give a reason; a pick outside the list, a duplicate, an ungrounded reason
(one that names none of the candidates it cites) or a copied instruction is
refused. If nothing survives, the hunt is stored unranked (`NO_VALID_PICK`). The
proposed sessions are computed by code from the picks (`sessions.ts`): an
ELEMENT session when exactly one element leaves the field empty, else a KIND
session; depth is the field's level.

### The panel

Three new persona packs under `packs/personas/panel-*`, each with its own
`situations.json` (S1 premise→stakes, S2 plot→climax, S3 theme→counter
argument) — `personaSituations` reads a per-pack file when present, hashes it
into the set hash and scans it at load — its own `evals.json` (six cases) and a
structural record. They are hidden from the ordinary persona picker and the
defaults, and offered only by the Panel preset. Questions are grouped by
`groupByPersona` (pure) in the session and on the review page.

### Compaction

The state block is built by code from rows, never from the summary: the
session's scope, the packs it pinned, every answer of the compacted rounds
verbatim, every accepted change verbatim, and the refusals by code. The summary
is one `callStructured` call per compacted range (stored once in
`InterviewCompaction`, unique per session and range, append-only), validated
(`validateRoundsSummary`: not empty, no copied instruction, no nine-word run of
an answer — the answers are already verbatim beside it) and fenced as the
model's own text. Any failure leaves the round uncompacted: compaction never
fails a round.

## Decisions

- **On demand, never nightly.** The stress test costs a deep call over a large
  input, and its answer is only useful when the author is there to act on it;
  running it beside 4.2's nightly check would spend the ceiling on reports
  nobody reads. The gap hunt is also on demand, so 4.2's nightly share does
  not apply to it; its result is kept until the bible moves (`bookMark`, a
  version count) and the page says when it is out of date.
- **A session from a stress test is a SELECTION**, not a new scope: the cited
  elements plus the subjects of cited facts (at most
  `STRESS_SESSION_ELEMENTS_MAX`), so 3.3's scope rules, packs and reconcile all
  apply unchanged. The weak points ride as a data block, and their fields lead
  the round as challenges, through the same focus 4.2 built for stale flags.
- **Citations are tags, not ids.** The model never sees a database id; it
  cites the tags it was shown, and code maps them back. An unknown tag refuses
  the whole weak point, because a weak point resting on nothing is the claim
  rule 2 forbids.
- **The panel is new keys**, `panel-*`, rather than reusing the existing
  persona keys with new names: the existing personas keep their packs and
  records, and a panel session's pins name packs a non-panel session never
  mounts.
- **Compaction is off by default until 4.4** (`COMPACTION_ON_BY_DEFAULT`). A
  session today is at most six rounds of at most 80 questions, whose answers
  fit the question prompt with room to spare; turning compaction on now would
  add a model call and a summary to sessions that do not need one. 4.4's
  "until I stop" sessions are what need it; the prompt, its evaluation set and
  record, the table and the tests are in place, so turning it on is one line.
- **Evals has one `book_level` input**, not three, to stay inside GitHub's ten
  dispatch inputs; `packs:evaluate -- --book-level` writes the three records.
- **Gap hunts keep the newest five a book.** Only the newest is read; older
  rows are deleted as a new one is stored. Stress tests are kept with the book:
  each was a confirmed, paid run, and sessions name the one they came from.
- **The panel checkbox's help sits outside its label**, so the control is named
  "Panel" alone; inside the label, its help (which names the three personas)
  made every persona checkbox's name ambiguous.
