# 2026-11-20 — 3.4 Word search

**Subphase:** Phase 3.4 (roadmap section 4, placed before 3.H), owner decision
D21 (option A, decrypt and scan), with D12 (English first) and rule 4 (author
text is private).

Word search within one book, with English stemming. The book's bible is
encrypted at rest, so there is nothing in the database to search: each search
reads the book inside the workspace's own context, the encryption extension
decrypts it, and the words are cut, stemmed and ranked in memory. Nothing new
is stored. A blind index (option B) is deferred by the owner.

## Design

```
BookBoard ─ <BookSearch>  (debounced; Enter searches at once; a newer query aborts the older)
   │  POST /api/w/:slug/books/:bookId/search  { "q": "…" }        ← a body, never ?q=
   ▼
route: withApiErrorHandling(requirePermission('bible.view'), { rateLimit: API_READ_LIMIT, scope book-search })
   ▼
searchBook (bible.view; BookSearchRequest, strict, raw-bounded)
   [tx, app_user, RLS] requireBook (live books only) → parseQuery (refusals by name)
        → readSearchDocuments: countByKind · elements by name (cap+1) · relations with notes (cap+1)
          · fields 250 elements at a time, scale keys skipped, until the text budget
          · every source → plain text (HtmlTextReader for rich text; tidy for the rest)
   [after commit] rankDocuments: needles → skip sources that cannot match → Intl.Segmenter
        → match by stem / surface / exact case / phrase → BM25F → top 50 → snippets
   ▼
BookSearchView { hits[{ element, matches[{ source, field, snippet{text,start,highlights} }] }],
                 totalHits, truncated, coverage{elements, searchedElements, complete, cut} }
```

**Documents.** A result is an element (or the book itself). An element's
sources: name, custom label, summary, every stored field (rich text through
the HTML reader, a list's items one a line, a choice's option label — the
words the author picked and sees — and no scale: a number from 1 to 5 is not
text), and the notes of its relations. A relation note belongs to BOTH ends,
labelled with the other end's name, because either end is where an author
would look for it. The book's own document is its title and logline.

**Analysis** (`src/lib/search/analyse.ts`). Words come from `Intl.Segmenter`
(`granularity: 'word'`, `isWordLike`) with their offsets in the plain text. A
word is folded: NFKC, every apostrophe spelling as `'`, format characters
dropped, diacritics folded through NFD (plus a short table for letters with
no mark to strip: ß, æ, œ, ø, ł, đ, ð, þ, ı) and the Greek final sigma read as
sigma, lower case, possessive stripped. It yields a SURFACE form and a STEM
(the irregular table, then Porter2); a capitalised word not at a sentence
start (any capital in a name-like source: a name, a label, the title) also
keeps its EXACT case-kept form.

**Matching and ranking** (`rank.ts`). Unquoted query words are optional and
match by stem; a token's weight says how closely: 1.3 when a capitalised query
word meets its exact form, 1.0 for the surface form, 0.6 for the stem alone. A
quoted phrase (straight or curly quotes) is required and matches surface forms
in sequence; a quoted single word is required and matches its surface form.
Scoring is BM25F: per source class a weight (title and name 3, label 2.5,
summary and logline 1.5, field 1, relation note 0.8) and a length
normalisation (0.3 for name-like sources, 0.75 for prose), k1 = 1.2, Lucene's
always-positive IDF over the book's documents; phrases and quoted words are
scored as their own terms times 1.5. Ties fall to the name, then the id.

**Skipping what cannot match.** A source is tokenised only if its folded text
contains one of the query's needles: a word's stem minus its last two
characters, plus every irregular form whose lemma has that stem; a phrase's
first word; a quoted word. Two facts make this sound: every word's surface is
a substring of its text's folded form (the folds are per character; the final
sigma, the one context-dependent lower-casing, is folded away), and Porter2
never alters more than the last two characters of a stem relative to the word
(the case analysis: `ying`→`ie` and `biliti`→`ble` are the widest; the golden
test checks it over all 42,649 words of Snowball's vocabulary). The unit suite
runs twenty queries both ways and compares the results exactly.

**Snippets** are plain text, at most 180 characters, cut at spaces around the
first match with 50 characters of lead, carrying merged highlight ranges as
offsets into the snippet and `start` placing it in the source. The client
builds `<mark>` elements from the ranges as React text.

## Decisions

- **POST, not `GET ?q=`.** The query is author text and a URL is not private:
  Next's own `BaseServer.handleRequest` span records `http.target` (the path
  WITH its query string) on whatever tracer provider is registered — ours is,
  when `OTEL_ENABLED=true` — and a reverse proxy's access log records the URI
  by default. Our own proxy and wrapper log only the pathname, and Sentry
  strips the query (`redactUrl`), but two of the sinks are outside our code.
  No layer logs a request body (`withApiErrorHandling` never does). The page
  never puts the query in its own URL either. The route reads nothing it
  changes, so it is charged to `API_READ_LIMIT` (120 a minute) through the
  wrapper's own option rather than the mutation tier; the edge counts a POST
  in the handler, not as a read.
- **Golden data from Snowball itself.** The task assumed no network; the
  session had it, so the real `voc.txt` / `output.txt` were fetched at a
  pinned snowball-data commit and committed unmodified (with `COPYING`), and
  the stemmer was written from `english.sbl` at a pinned snowball commit (the
  snowballstem.org page answered 403 here; the `.sbl` is what that page is
  generated from). It is the CURRENT English algorithm, so it carries the
  revisions after the classic Porter2 description (more region prefixes, the
  `-ing` exceptions, `-ogist`, `past` as a short syllable). All 42,649 words
  match. `golden-pin.json` ties `PORTER2_VERSION` to both files' SHA-256.
- **Irregular forms are few, and only unambiguous ones.** About 160 forms of
  about 120 lemmas. A form that is as often another word (left, saw, rose,
  fell, lay, bore, bit, lit, dove, ground, bound, wound, lives, leaves, does)
  is out, as are comparatives (better → good): a wrong merge makes search
  find the wrong word silently. The test holds every entry to changing a
  stem, having no chain, and being folded and no stop word.
- **Stop words only for unquoted query words**, and only when something else
  is left, so "the" alone still searches; documents are never filtered, so a
  phrase like "of the" still matches.
- **Phrases and quoted words are required; unquoted words are optional.** That
  is what quotes mean in a search box, and it gives quotes a meaning beyond a
  ranking nudge; the phrase is still a signal among the elements that hold it.
- **Exact case is a signal, never a filter.** "Hope" finds the noun too; it
  only ranks the name first. Sentence starts are excluded from exact forms in
  prose (a capital there says nothing), but not in names and titles.
- **Length is a word count, not the token count.** BM25's length
  normalisation reads each source's count of letter-or-digit runs, taken for
  every source, so a skipped source still counts towards the averages without
  being segmented. It approximates the Segmenter's word count closely enough
  for a normalisation constant, and it is the same count everywhere.
- **Reading: narrow and in chunks.** Elements and relation notes are read with
  only the columns search uses (`listForSearch`, `listNotesForBook`, which
  asks the database only WHETHER a note is set); fields are read 250 elements
  at a time, skipping keys that are scales in every template that declares
  them (`NON_TEXT_FIELD_KEYS`; `prominence` is a scale in one template and a
  choice in another, so it is read), and reading stops at 8 million characters
  of plain text. Each cut is named in `coverage` (`ELEMENTS_CAP`,
  `RELATIONS_CAP`, `TEXT_BUDGET`) and shown. Ranking happens after the
  transaction commits, so the connection is not held while words are counted.
- **Language.** `Book` has no language column, so every book is analysed as
  English (`searchLanguageOf(null)`); the other-language analyser (surface
  matching, no stemming, no stop words) exists and is tested, and is what a
  future `Book.language` selects.
- **Not searched yet: interview answers and research.** Both are author text
  and both are cheap to add as sources, but each belongs to a session or a run
  rather than an element, so a result would need a second kind of link and a
  second kind of row; they are the next candidates. Version history and
  enrichment proposals are not searched: they are past or proposed states, and
  matching them would report text the bible no longer holds. Story-map sticky
  notes (3.1's freeform layer) are another candidate once 3.2 gives them a
  place to link to.
- **The board, not the command palette.** The palette is mounted above the
  workspace provider and has no book in context; a book-scoped search fits the
  board, where results link straight into the elements. A palette entry is
  left for later.
- **No help-site page.** `site/manifest.json` publishes no section yet (only
  the landing page), so there is no pattern to follow.

## Performance

Counted work is what CI holds (`book-search-work.test.ts`, `rank.test.ts`):
twice the book is twice the sources read and the words segmented, stems are
cached per request, and a word the book does not hold segments nothing.

The wall clock is the load test's. Measured on the development container
(4 cores, load average 3 to 6 from other work) at the roadmap's full size,
2,000 elements and their fields, a search took 0.4 to 0.7 s: reading and
decrypting about 16,000 encrypted values (Prisma row materialisation plus one
AES-GCM open per value) was 300 to 450 ms of it, the plain-text conversion 50
to 100 ms, and the ranking 15 ms (a word the book does not hold) to 130 ms (a
word in nearly every field). That is above the 300 ms page budget the other
reads meet, so the load test holds search to its own 1,000 ms
(`WORD_SEARCH_P95_BOUND_MS`). Option A's cost is linear in the book by
design; a blind index (option B) is the way under the page budget if the owner
wants it.

`npm run test:load` (2,000 elements, 20,000 versions, 30 runs, the same busy
container): search for a word in nearly every field p50 459 ms, p95 603 ms;
for a name p50 471 ms, p95 589 ms (the other pages p95 3 to 78 ms). At CI's
400 elements both searches were p95 127 to 131 ms.
