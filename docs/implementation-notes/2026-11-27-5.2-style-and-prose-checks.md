# 2026-11-27 — 5.2 The style sheet and prose checks (D3, D34)

**Commit:** uncommitted (branch `wip/5.2`, cut from `main` at `04a5da5`)

Phase 5's second subphase: the STYLE element becomes a structured style sheet,
filled by the style interview pack, and compiled into deterministic prose
checks that run in the worker a few seconds after the author stops typing.
Their findings are margin notes the author dismisses or acts on; the product
never applies an edit, and a suggestion is a deterministic replacement the
author accepts in the editor. The owner's four decisions of 2026-11-27 are
recorded as D34 and the 5.2 text is amended to match.

## Design

```
style sheet   STYLE template + six fields: check_tense (past | present), check_person (first | second | third limited |
              third omniscient), check_spelling (British | American | Canadian | Australian), quote_marks (double |
              single | angled | dash | none), tag_punctuation (inside | outside), word_swaps ("avoid → use" list items);
              avoided_words (existing) is the banned list — items of one to four words are flagged
              per-character voice notes: CHARACTER `voiced_against` STYLE (voice_departures, voice_words), declared on
              CHARACTER (the earlier kind), interviewed by the relations pack; not compiled (no rule can read prose)
              the book's sheet: oldest whole-book STYLE element, else oldest with no span (pickStyleSheetElement)
interview     the craft-style pack covers the six fields (examples for the check tense and the tag comma); records
              regenerated (`npm run packs:evaluate`); the relations pack covers the two voice fields
engine        sceneProseText (3.4's HtmlTextReader + italic runs aligned beside it) → boundText → speechRanges (the
              sheet's marks) → segment (Intl.Segmenter sentences per line, 3.4's analyser words) → rules → scope filter
              (NARRATION rules never touch speech) → per-rule and per-scene bounds → sentence keys
rules         original: filter-words, overused-words (book baseline), repeated-openers, similar-names, tense-slip,
              person-slip, banned-words (+ swaps), spelling-variant, dialogue-tag-punctuation; readability per scene
              ported:   write-good → intensifiers (deletion offered), wordy (multi-word only), cliches;
                        retext → repeated-words (deletion offered); alex's plugins → inclusive-language (off by default)
run           save (text changed) → scheduleProseCheck after commit → BullMQ `prose-check` {workspaceId, sceneId},
              delay 4 s, deduplication { id: prose-check-<sceneId>, reschedule, keepLastIfActive }
              worker: chargeProseCheckRun (60/min/workspace, else requeued) → READ current text, sheet, settings,
              characters, baseline (recomputed when 30 min old, encrypted) → CHECK (no transaction) → STORE under the
              scene's advisory lock iff the text still keys as checked (else SUPERSEDED) → replace check + findings
read          GET …/scenes/:id/prose-checks → status (NOT_CHECKED | PENDING | OUTDATED | CHECKED), readability, findings
              minus rules off and dismissals, each with `matched` (its text in the scene's current plain text)
editor        use-prose-notes (read on open, after each save; ask for a check when never checked / outdated) →
              SceneTextEditor places each finding by buildEditorTextMap + placeRange (only where `matched` holds) →
              ProseNotes plugin: decorations that map through edits; the open note's suggestion drawn (the suggestion
              mark); Accept = tr.insertText / tr.delete → onChange → autosave; Reject = dismiss
margin        ProseNotesMargin: a list of buttons aligned to their lines on wide screens, stacked on narrow ones; a
              filter by rule; status, readability; Dismiss, Turn rule off, Accept, Reject; a polite live status
settings      ProseCheckSettings on the manuscript page: the sheet in use, every rule on/off, alex on/off, dismissed
```

## Decisions

- **The structured sheet is new fields beside the old ones, not retyped
  ones.** `tense` and `spelling_variant` stay prose: stored books hold free text
  there, and retyping them to a choice would refuse every existing value at
  its next save (2.H's `FIELD_TYPE_CHANGED`). The check fields are choices the
  checks read exactly; the prose fields go on describing the style. The
  existing `avoided_words` list is the banned list (an item of one to four
  words is checked; a construction is counted as not checkable), so a book that
  already listed words gets the check at once.
- **Person sits on the style sheet, beside point of view.** The roadmap names
  person in the sheet; the POV entry's `person` stays prose about perspective.
  The template and pack descriptions now say the sheet holds the person the
  checks hold the narration to, and leave perspective and distance to POV.
- **Swaps are list items, not a new field type.** "avoid → use" (also `->`,
  `=>`) parsed by the compiler, found by search, not by a pattern; an item that
  does not parse is counted. A pairs type would have touched the template
  schema, `normaliseFieldValue`, the bundle and the PDF for no gain.
- **Voice notes are a relation, declared on CHARACTER.** A character's voice
  is held against one sheet; the relation's prose fields are interviewed by
  the relations pack (as 3.3's relation fields are). No rule reads them.
- **One sheet a book.** A part-scoped sheet is not applied to the whole
  manuscript: its tense or spelling would be held against scenes it does not
  govern. Several part sheets per scene is later work.
- **alex's rules, not the alex package.** alex 11 brings a command-line stack
  (unified-engine, update-notifier) whose glob and minimatch carry moderate
  advisories, which the audit gate refuses. Its rules are `retext-equality` and
  `retext-profanities`, run with profanity sureness "likely" (alex's own
  filter), narration only, and with personal pronouns never flagged (in fiction
  "he" and "she" are characters; every one would be a note).
- **Debounced by BullMQ, not by a job id.** A custom per-scene `jobId` is
  ignored while a job holds it — a save during a running check, or after one
  retained as completed, would never be checked. BullMQ 6.7's deduplication
  with `reschedule` (a waiting check moves to after the newest save) and
  `keepLastIfActive` (a save during a run is queued once it finishes) is the
  debounce; the job reads the scene's CURRENT text, so the payload is ids only.
- **The work cap is in the worker.** A save that only reschedules a waiting
  check costs nothing; a check about to run charges the workspace's minute, and
  past it goes back on the queue for when the minute frees (never dropped,
  never run). In-process, like the model-call limit.
- **Validity is the text, not the revision.** A details save moves the
  revision without touching the text; the check keys the text it read
  (`textKey`, HMAC) and stands while the scene's text keys the same. The store
  refuses a check whose text has moved under the scene's advisory lock (the one
  saves take), so no stale finding is ever stored.
- **Nothing in clear.** A finding row is offsets, a rule and a keyed sentence
  hash; its message parameters and suggestion (words of the scene or the
  sheet) are its encrypted `detail`. The read hands the editor each note's
  `matched` text from the scene's current text (the response carries author
  text to its author, as the scene GET does), and the editor places a note
  only where its own text reads the same.
- **Rules off and dismissals at read.** The engine runs every rule the sheet
  allows; the read hides what the book turned off and what was dismissed, so
  turning a rule back on, or clearing dismissals, shows the notes at once, with
  no re-check.
- **Decorations, not marks.** The suggestion-mark pattern is drawn with
  ProseMirror decorations (struck-through range, replacement widget). A real
  mark would be serialised into the stored HTML — which the prose profile
  would refuse, and which would put a product artefact into the author's
  text. Accepting is an ordinary transaction; the autosave sends it.
- **Conservative rules.** Filter words and tense slips fire only right after a
  subject; tense lists are closed; a modal blocks a tense slip; speech and
  italics are skipped; person is checked only for a third-person sheet;
  write-good's single-word "wordy" entries and its non-intensifier weasel words
  are dropped; similar names fire only when both names are in the scene. 5.H's
  sample decides what is retuned next.
- **Readability is a number and a band, never a verdict,** over the whole
  scene (speech included), not assessed under a hundred words.

- **Review fixes (independent review, same day).** The job's store locks the
  scene's ROW before replacing its check (a chapter or part deletion takes the
  scene row, then the check, and no scene lock: the other order deadlocked);
  a dismissal is an insert that skips a duplicate (an empty-update upsert raced
  to a unique violation on a double click); a suggestion carries the text it
  covered (`matched`) and is drawn only where the editor still reads it, never
  over a mention atom (accepting would have replaced the whole mention); a
  mention's `@Name` takes no note but similar names; avoided words are cut into
  words by the scene's own word rules (a hyphenated item could never match);
  the generic "you" is no subject unless the sheet is second person; a run of
  three of one word is a voice's emphasis; with no sheet, straight single marks
  are read as speech too; a checked scene of no words says nothing was checked.
  `PROSE_RULES_VERSION` 1 records the new hash (no check was ever stored under
  the old one outside a test database).

## Deferred

- Several style sheets in one book (per part, per viewpoint) applied to their
  own scenes.
- The dash-speech convention's narration interruptions ("— Yes, she said —")
  read as speech; a plural possessive closes single-quoted speech early. Both
  are stated in `dialogue.ts`.
- The per-character voice notes are not compiled into a check.
- 5.H item 3 (sample 500 findings, retune under 70 %) and item 2's budget work
  are 5.H's.

## Bundle budget

No budget was raised: both routes stay inside their recorded budgets. The scene
editor grew by about 5.1 kB gzipped (490,091 → 495,234 bytes first load: the
margin, its hook, the client-safe vocabulary and the editor map; the TipTap
extension rides the editor's dynamic chunk) and the manuscript outline by about
1.9 kB (472,131 → 474,014: the settings section). Neither route carries retext,
write-good or alex: the engine is imported only by the worker's job, and the
routes' usecases read the setup and the stored findings without it.
