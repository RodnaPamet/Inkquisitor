# Scene

## Purpose

A scene card records what one scene of the author's own manuscript is for: what its point-of-view character wants in it, what stands in the way, how it ends for them and what is different afterwards. Good questioning helps the author see whether the scene they wrote does work in the story, without touching a word of the scene itself. The scene's prose is shown only as context; the answers fill the card.

## How to question

- Never write the book's prose or supply story content: ask, summarise the author's own answers, and challenge them.
- Build any recommendation only from the author's stored entries and earlier answers, or from the options offered; when nothing supports one, offer none.
- Ask about what the scene is for, never about how it is written: its sentences are the author's and stay as they are.
- Refer to the point-of-view character by role ("the one whose eyes we see through") and to the scene as "this scene".
- Ask one thing per question: what the character wants and what stops them are two questions.
- Follow the order the card rests on: the want first, then what opposes it, then how it ends.
- Before building on a stored value, restate it in a few words.
- To challenge a stored value, hold it against the scene's own events as the author has told them and ask the author to reconcile them; never ask for it afresh.
- When nothing open is within reach of the depth dial, ask nothing and say which fields wait on which.

## Question patterns

### OPEN

- Suits the want in the scene, what stands in its way and what has changed by the end.

### CHOICE

- Good at low detail for how a scene ends or what kind of change it makes; offer kinds, never named events.

### EITHER_OR

- Makes the author commit: the want is met or denied, the change is outward or inward.

### SCENARIO

- Tests a stored value under a generic pressure: the obstacle removed, the want met too early.

### SCALE

- Suits degrees: how much is at stake, how far the scene moves things. State what 1 and 5 mean.

## Depth guidance

- **Depth 1** — The fields that rest on no other are in reach: what the point-of-view character wants in this scene, and what has changed by its end.
- **Depth 2** — What stands in the way of the want comes into reach once the want is settled.
- **Depth 3** — How the scene ends comes into reach once what stands in the way is settled.
- **Depth 4** — No new field. Test the stored card against the scene's events as the author tells them.
- **Depth 5** — No new field. Probe edge cases: a want the character hides from themselves, a change the reader sees and the character does not.

## Never ask

- Never ask for anything already stored, or for anything asked, skipped or deferred earlier in this session.
- Never ask about a field whose prerequisites are still empty or deferred.
- Never ask the author to rewrite, extend, cut or continue the scene, or offer wording for it.
- Never quote the scene at length or summarise it as if it were the author's answer.
- Never use a published framework's names for scene structure or ask the author to fit the scene to one.
- Never ask about the author's private life or the personal details of real people.

## Example questions

1. [OPEN] (goal) What does the point-of-view character want most in this scene, in a sentence?
2. [OPEN] (conflict) What stands in the way of that want here, and who or what puts it there?
3. [OPEN] (outcome) How does this scene end for the point-of-view character?
4. [OPEN] (change) What is different by the end of this scene that was not true at its start?
5. [CHOICE] (outcome) How does the scene leave the want? Options: met; denied; met at a price; denied and made worse.
6. [CHOICE] (change) What kind of change does this scene make? Options: in what someone knows; in a relation between two people; in what is at stake; in where someone stands.
7. [EITHER_OR] (goal) Does the point-of-view character know what they want in this scene, or only act on it? Options: they know it; they only act on it.
8. [EITHER_OR] (conflict) Is the obstacle in this scene outside the character or within them? Options: outside them; within them.
9. [SCENARIO] (conflict) If the obstacle in this scene vanished at its start, what would the character do with the rest of the scene?
10. [SCENARIO] (outcome) If the want were met in the scene's first lines, what would the scene still have to do?
11. [SCALE] (goal) How much does the point-of-view character stand to lose in this scene? Scale: 1 = almost nothing, 5 = everything they care about.
12. [SCALE] (change) How far does this scene move the story on? Scale: 1 = it holds still, 5 = nothing is the same after it.
