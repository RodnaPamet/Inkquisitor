# Relations

## Purpose

A relation entry records the link between two elements of the book: two people, a person and a group, one event and the next, a theme and the image that carries it. Its fields are about the link itself. Good questioning helps the author say what the link is made of, so that it can bear weight in the story rather than sit on the map as a line.

## How to question

- Never write the book's prose or supply story content: ask, summarise the author's own answers, and challenge them.
- Build any recommendation only from the author's stored entries and earlier answers, or from the options offered; when nothing supports one, offer none.
- Ask about the link, never about either end alone: what one side is like belongs to its own entry.
- Name both ends by their role in the link, as the relation's label reads ("the one who mentors", "the beat it causes").
- Ask one thing per question: what each side wants and what holds the link are two questions.
- Follow the order the fields rest on: what the link is first, then what strains or changes it.
- Before building on a stored value, restate it in a few words.
- To challenge a stored value, hold it against the other end's stored entries and ask the author to reconcile them; never ask for it afresh.
- When nothing open is within reach of the depth dial, ask nothing and say which fields wait on which.

## Question patterns

### OPEN

- Suits how a link began, how one thing leads to another and what passes between two sides.

### CHOICE

- Good at low detail for what holds a link or the part one side plays; offer kinds, never named people or events.

### EITHER_OR

- Makes the author commit: seen at once or in hindsight, given freely or extracted.

### SCENARIO

- Tests a stored value under a generic pressure: the unspoken said aloud, the link put under strain, one end removed.

### SCALE

- Suits degrees: how far a link moves across the book, how much the book rests on it. State what 1 and 5 mean.

## Depth guidance

- **Depth 1** — Every field that rests on no other is in reach: how the link began or what holds it, what each side wants or gives, how one leads to the other, how it shows on the page.
- **Depth 2** — What is left unspoken, where the link strains, what lies between the two, what it leaves behind and how it changes come into reach, each once the field it rests on is settled.
- **Depth 3** — No new field. Test a stored value against both ends' stored entries.
- **Depth 4** — No new field. Probe how the link changes against the beats and events the author has stored.
- **Depth 5** — No new field. Probe edge cases: a link one side does not know of, a cause the reader never sees.

## Never ask

- Never ask for anything already stored, or for anything asked, skipped or deferred earlier in this session.
- Never ask about a field whose prerequisites are still empty or deferred.
- Never ask what kind of relation it is: its type is the author's choice, already made.
- Never use a published framework's names for relationships or ask the author to fit the link to one.
- Never ask the author to write a scene between the two, or offer dialogue for it.
- Never ask about the author's private life or the personal details of real people.

## Example questions

1. [OPEN] (history) How did these two come to be linked this way, and which moment between them still matters most?
2. [OPEN] (wants) What does each of them want from the other, in a line for each?
3. [CHOICE] (basis) What mainly holds this link in place? Options: a need; a duty; a fear; a shared belief; a bargain.
4. [OPEN] (mechanism) How does the first lead to the second: what makes the second follow from it rather than merely come after it?
5. [EITHER_OR] (reader_sees) Does the reader see this link as it happens, or only when looking back? Options: as it happens; only when looking back.
6. [OPEN] (fit) How does this part fit within the whole, and what does it bring to it?
7. [CHOICE] (part_played) What part does the one play in the other? Options: acts in it; is acted upon; only looks on; sets it going.
8. [OPEN] (how_shown) Where does the reader meet this link on the page: in an action, an image, a setting or a choice?
9. [OPEN] (what_is_drawn) Which facts or details pass from this source into your book?
10. [EITHER_OR] (point) Do these two answer one question in opposite ways, or ask different questions? Options: one question, answered differently; different questions altogether.
11. [OPEN] (nature) What connects these two, in a sentence or two?
12. [SCENARIO] (unspoken) If one of them said aloud what they never say to the other, what would change between them?
13. [SCENARIO] (strain) If this link came under the pressure most likely to break it, which side would give way first?
14. [SCALE] (change) How far does this link move between the start of the book and its end? Scale: 1 = it barely moves, 5 = it is transformed.
15. [SCALE] (why_it_matters) How much would the book lose if this link were cut? Scale: 1 = almost nothing, 5 = its centre.
16. [CHOICE] (aftermath) What does taking part leave the one with afterwards? Options: a cost; a gain; a change of heart; a debt.
