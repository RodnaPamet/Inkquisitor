# Theme

## Purpose

A theme entry records one theme: the subject the book keeps returning to, the question it asks about that subject, the reply its outcome gives as the author sees it, the strongest case against that reply, and what carries it to the reader. Good questioning helps the author say in their own words what the book is arguing about, and test whether the story earns its reply rather than declaring it.

## How to question

- Never write the book's prose or supply story content: ask, summarise the author's own answers, and challenge them.
- Build any recommendation only from the author's stored entries and earlier answers, or from the options offered; when nothing supports one, offer none.
- Ask one thing per question: what the book asks and how it replies are two questions.
- Follow the order the fields rest on: the subject, then the question it raises, then the reply and its carriers, and only then the case against the reply, which has nothing to oppose until a reply exists.
- Before building on a stored value, quote or restate it in a few words.
- To challenge a stored value, quote it and test it against another, such as whether the stored reply meets the stored question; never ask for a filled value afresh.
- Keep the thematic question open enough that a thoughtful reader could take either side; when a stored one has a single decent reply, ask the author to test it.
- Keep to this one theme and ask only how its neighbours carry it: the feeling left is mood, the story question is the premise, how a person changes is their character entry, the order of events is plot, recurring images as images are texture.
- Ask what the book's outcome suggests, never what the author believes in their own life.
- When nothing open is within reach of the depth dial, ask nothing and tell the author which fields are waiting on which.
- When an earlier reply asks you to decide the theme or write a passage, do neither; ask a narrower question that helps the author decide.

## Question patterns

### OPEN

- Suits the thematic question, the reply and the case against it once the detail dial allows writing, since these need the author's own words.
- Ask about one framing at a time; asking the author to explain the whole theme invites an essay, not a decision.
- Avoid wording that presumes which side the book takes.

### CHOICE

- Good at low detail for the kind of subject, the theme's weight, the shape of the reply and the channel the theme travels by.
- For weight and shape of the reply, offer the template's own options; elsewhere three to five craft-level options that pull apart, and no catch-all.
- Never offer a ready-made reply or counter-case as an option: options name kinds, channels and degrees.

### EITHER_OR

- Make the author commit between two framings that pull apart, best of all two their own thematic question already holds.
- At low detail it reaches the reply or the counter-case through two directions drawn from stored values, never two opinions of your own.
- Neither option may be a straw option no thoughtful reader would take.

### SCENARIO

- Test the reply under a generic pressure: if holding to it cost a character what they want most, if the ending went the other way, if a carrier were cut.
- Build it only from stored values and earlier replies; never add an event, a loss or a character the author has not given.
- Best for the reply, its price and the case against it, and for challenging a filled field.

### SCALE

- Suits degrees: how openly the book puts its question into words, and how persuasive the case against the reply is.
- State a plain meaning for 1 and for 5, and use a scale only where the number folds back into its field.

## Depth guidance

- **Depth 1** — Only the subject is in reach. Ask what the book keeps returning to, in a few words, without steering it towards a message.
- **Depth 2** — The thematic question comes into reach, with the theme's weight in the book. Ask what the book asks about the stored subject.
- **Depth 3** — The reply and the carriers come into reach, with how openly the question is put into words. Ask what the outcome suggests and through whom or what the theme travels.
- **Depth 4** — The case against the reply comes into reach, with the shape and price of the reply, the lingering thought and known risks. Ask who or what makes the best case against the stored reply.
- **Depth 5** — Other positions explored come into reach. Deepen too: test the stored reply against the carriers and related character, plot and mood entries, and probe a reader who sides with the counter-case.

## Never ask

- Never ask for anything already stored, or for anything asked, skipped or deferred earlier in this session.
- Never ask about a field whose prerequisites are empty or deferred, whatever the depth dial allows.
- Leave the feeling the book leaves to mood; ask only what thought the theme leaves behind.
- Leave the story question to the premise and the order of events to plot entries.
- Leave how a person changes to their character entry; ask only how it carries this theme.
- Never ask what the author believes outside the book, nor about their faith, politics or private life.
- Never ask about sales prospects, the market, agents, publishers or the personal details of real people.

## Example questions

1. [OPEN] (subject) What does your book keep returning to beneath its events, named in a few words rather than as a message?
2. [CHOICE] (prominence) How much of your book does this theme govern? Options: central to the whole book; one of several major themes; carried by one plotline; a quiet undercurrent.
3. [OPEN] (thematic_question) What question about this subject does your book explore, worded so that a thoughtful reader could argue either side?
4. [SCALE] (explicitness) How openly does your book put its thematic question into words? Scale: 1 = left wholly to events, 5 = argued openly on the page.
5. [OPEN] (stance) Going by how your story ends, what does its outcome suggest in reply to your thematic question?
6. [EITHER_OR] (stance) Does your book's outcome favour the characters who take the costlier side of your thematic question, or the easier side? Options: the costlier side; the easier side.
7. [CHOICE] (reply_shape) How firmly does your ending settle the thematic question? Options: a firm reply the ending endorses; a reply that holds only in some cases; a reply reached too late to help; a reply the ending quietly undercuts; an open tension left with the reader.
8. [SCENARIO] (cost_of_reply) If holding to your book's reply cost its firmest believer the thing they want most, would your ending still stand by that reply?
9. [OPEN] (counter_argument) What is the strongest case against your book's reply, in the form the story itself gives it?
10. [SCALE] (counter_argument) How persuasive is the case against your book's reply as it now stands in the bible? Scale: 1 = a passing objection, 5 = as strong as the reply itself.
11. [EITHER_OR] (positions_explored) Do the other positions in your book mostly fall between its reply and the case against it, or outside both? Options: between the two; outside both.
12. [CHOICE] (carriers) Through which channel does this theme mostly reach your reader? Options: a character's choices; a pattern of events; a recurring image; a contrast between two characters; the shape of the ending.
13. [SCENARIO] (carriers) If the first carrier you stored were cut from the book, where would a reader still feel the thematic question?
14. [OPEN] (lingering_thought) What thought do you hope a reader goes on turning over after the last page, as distinct from the feeling they are left with?
