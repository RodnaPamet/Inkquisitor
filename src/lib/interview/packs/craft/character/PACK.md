# Character

## Purpose

A character entry records one person or person-like being: the job they do for the story, the goal they knowingly chase, the past hurt and the untrue belief it left behind, what they actually lack, and how they change or refuse to. Good questioning helps the author put each of these in their own words, see where the want and the need pull apart, and catch a turn nothing in the entry explains.

## How to question

- Never write the book's prose or supply story content: ask, summarise the author's own answers, and challenge them.
- Build any recommendation only from the author's stored entries and earlier answers, or from the options offered; when nothing supports one, offer none.
- Ask one thing per question. The want and the wound are separate fields, and so are a trait and its cause; never fold two of them into one question.
- Follow the order the fields rest on: the role first, then the want and the wound, then the false belief that grows from the wound, then the need, which rests on the want and that belief, then the arc.
- Before building on a stored value, quote or restate it in a few words, so the author extends their own material rather than starting cold.
- To challenge a stored value, quote it and ask the author to test it against another stored entry, such as a want the stored wound makes hard to believe; never ask for it afresh as if it were empty.
- Reach the inner life through behaviour the author can picture: what this character does or avoids under pressure.
- Keep to this one character: ask how they meet a faction, a plotline or a theme, never about that other element itself.
- Keep every option craft-level: kinds of hurt, directions, degrees. An option never names a person, place, event, relationship or past fact the author has not given.
- When an earlier reply asks you to decide or write something for the author, do not do it; ask a narrower question that helps them decide for themselves.

## Question patterns

### OPEN

- Best for the wound, the false belief, the need and the arc, where only the author's own words will do.
- Word it so it presumes nothing: not that the wound is one event, not that the character is likeable or wicked.
- Shape: "What does your protagonist go after, in the words they would use for it?"

### CHOICE

- Good at low detail for the role, the source of the wound and the trait a reader meets first.
- Offer three to five craft-level options that pull apart, and no catch-all.
- For the direction of change, offer the options as the template words them.

### EITHER_OR

- Use it to make the author commit between two readings: a want admitted or denied, a hurt done to the character or done by them.
- Build both sides from stored material or a plain craft contrast; neither may be a straw option.
- Shape: "Is the hurt that shaped your protagonist something done to them, or something they did?"

### SCENARIO

- Test a stored value under a generic pressure: the want granted early, the wound made public, the belief proved wrong.
- Build the hypothetical only from what is stored or already said, never from a new person or event.
- Best for the false belief, the need and the arc, and for challenging a filled value.

### SCALE

- Suits degrees: how far the character forces events, how far a reader should side with them, how raw the old hurt still is.
- Give a plain meaning for 1 and for 5 every time.
- On a text field, use a scale only to measure a degree of what the field holds.

## Depth guidance

- **Depth 1** — Only the role is in reach. Keep to the job this character does for the story, and leave what they want or have suffered for later.
- **Depth 2** — The want and the wound come into reach, both resting on the role, with defining traits and physical presence. Ask the want and the wound separately, and press for a want that can be won or lost.
- **Depth 3** — The false belief comes into reach once the wound is settled, with speaking voice, personal stakes, drive to act and reader sympathy. Probe what the wound taught this character that is untrue.
- **Depth 4** — The need comes into reach once the want and the false belief are both settled. Probe the gap between what this character chases and what they lack, quoting both stored values.
- **Depth 5** — The arc and the direction of change come into reach once the need is settled. Ask what the change or the refusal costs, and test it against the stored want, wound and need.

## Never ask

- Never ask for anything already stored, or for anything asked, skipped or deferred earlier in this session.
- Never ask about a field whose prerequisites are empty or deferred: no false belief before the wound, no need before the want and the false belief, no arc before the need.
- Leave the group this character belongs to, and what that group is for, to its faction entry.
- Leave how dialogue is laid out on the page to the prose style entry, and whose eyes the narration uses to the point of view entry.
- Leave the order of events to plot entries: ask how this character meets an event the author has given, never what happens next.
- Never ask whether a real person inspired the character, or about the author's own private life and hurts.
- Never ask about sales prospects, agents, publishers or publishing logistics.

## Example questions

1. [CHOICE] (role) Which job does this character mainly do for your story? Options: carries the main plotline; stands in the protagonist's way; helps the protagonist along; throws the protagonist's values into contrast; adds pressure from the edges.
2. [EITHER_OR] (defining_traits) Which does a reader notice first about this character, how they treat people or how they meet trouble? Options: how they treat people; how they meet trouble.
3. [OPEN] (want) What does this character knowingly go after, stated as something they could win or lose?
4. [EITHER_OR] (want) Would this character admit to their want if asked outright, or keep it to themselves? Options: admit to it; keep it to themselves.
5. [SCALE] (agency) How far does this character make events happen while chasing the want you have stored? Scale: 1 = mostly reacts, 5 = mostly forces events.
6. [CHOICE] (wound) Where does the hurt that still shapes this character come from? Options: something done to them; something they did; something they failed to do; a loss nobody caused.
7. [SCALE] (wound) How sharply does the old hurt still cut when the story opens? Scale: 1 = long healed over, 5 = still raw.
8. [OPEN] (false_belief) What untrue lesson did this character take from the wound you have stored?
9. [SCENARIO] (false_belief) If the hurt you have stored happened to this character again tomorrow, what would they tell themselves about why?
10. [SCALE] (reader_sympathy) How fully should a reader side with this character for most of the book? Scale: 1 = kept at a cool distance, 5 = fully on their side.
11. [EITHER_OR] (need) Is what this character truly lacks something they could gain, or something they must come to accept? Options: something to gain; something to accept.
12. [SCENARIO] (want, need) If this character were handed their want in the opening chapters, which part of their trouble would it leave untouched?
13. [CHOICE] (arc_direction) Which way does this character move in relation to their need by the last page? Options: grows towards the need; turns away from the need; holds firm and changes others; changes, then slides back; stays essentially the same.
14. [OPEN] (arc) What does changing, or refusing to change, cost this character by the end of the book?
