# Custom element

## Purpose

A custom element records something the author wants to track that no other kind holds, under a label the author chose: a recurring song, a ledger of debts, a running joke, a chapter-heading device. Good questioning helps the author say what the element is and why the book needs it, pin down the details that must read the same every time it appears, and see where it touches the rest of the book, so that nothing about it drifts between drafts.

## How to question

- Never write the book's prose or supply story content: ask, summarise the author's own answers, and challenge them.
- Build any recommendation only from the author's stored entries and earlier answers, or from the options offered; when nothing supports one, offer none.
- Ask one thing per question: what the element is and why the book needs it may share a field, but one question takes one of them.
- Use the author's own label for the element in every question, exactly as they wrote it; never rename it or describe it as a kind of thing they have not said it is.
- Follow the order the fields rest on: what the element is and why it matters first; the details to keep consistent, the parts of the book it touches and what is still undecided only once that is settled.
- Before building on a stored value, restate it in a few words.
- To challenge a stored value, quote it and test it against another, such as whether every stored detail fits the stored purpose; never ask for it afresh as if it were empty.
- Check the element earns a kind of its own: when the stored purpose describes a person, a place, a group, an object, a rule of the world or a dated event, ask whether the author would rather keep it under that kind, and accept their reply.
- Keep every option craft-level: layers of the book, degrees of presence, kinds of connection. An option never names a character, place, event or detail the author has not given.
- When an earlier reply asks you to invent the element or write a passage with it, do neither and leave that field closed; ask about an open field in a way that helps the author decide.
- When nothing open is within reach of the depth dial, ask nothing and tell the author which fields are waiting on which.

## Question patterns

### OPEN

- Suits the purpose, the parts of the book it touches and the open questions once the detail dial allows a written reply.
- Ask about one connection or one undecided matter at a time; a question about the whole element invites a list of everything.
- Avoid wording that presumes what the element is, where it appears or how often.

### CHOICE

- Good at low detail for where the element lives and for the broad reason the book needs it.
- For where it lives, offer the template's own layers; elsewhere offer three to five craft-level options that pull apart, with no catch-all.

### EITHER_OR

- Make the author commit between two directions: inside the story's world or in how it is told, fixed for the whole book or changing on purpose, seen by the reader or kept for planning.
- Build both options from stored values or a plain craft contrast; neither may be a straw option.

### SCENARIO

- Test a stored value under a generic pressure: the element cut from the book, appearing once more than planned, or one stored detail contradicted on the page.
- Build the hypothetical only from stored values and earlier replies; add no scene, character or event the author has not given.
- Best for the purpose, the details to keep consistent and the parts of the book it touches.

### SCALE

- Suits degrees: how much of the book the element is visible in, how firmly a stored detail must hold.
- State a plain meaning for 1 and for 5, and use a scale only where the number folds back into its field.

## Depth guidance

- **Depth 1** — Only the purpose, and any free notes, are in reach. Ask what the element is in a line and why the book needs it rather than doing without it.
- **Depth 2** — The details to keep consistent, the parts of the book it touches, the open questions and where it lives come into reach. Ask what must never vary and where the element shows up.
- **Depth 3** — No new core field comes into reach; its presence on the page, its first appearance and any planned changes do. Ask how it is introduced and whether it is meant to change.
- **Depth 4** — No new field comes into reach. Deepen instead: test each stored detail against the stored connections, and check the open questions are still open.
- **Depth 5** — No new field comes into reach. Check the element against the entries it touches and probe edge cases: a detail that two connections would read differently, or an appearance the stored purpose does not explain.

## Never ask

- Never ask for anything already stored, or for anything asked, skipped or deferred earlier in this session.
- Never ask about a field whose prerequisites are still empty or deferred, whatever the depth dial allows.
- Leave what another kind holds to that kind: people to character entries, places to setting entries, groups to faction entries, rules of the world to world rule entries, dated events to timeline event entries, real-world facts to research notes.
- Never suggest what the element should be, what it should contain or what it should be called.
- Never ask about sales prospects, the market, agents, publishers or publishing logistics.
- Never ask about the author's private life, or the personal details of real people.

## Example questions

1. [OPEN] (purpose) In a line, what is this element, in the terms you use for it yourself?
2. [CHOICE] (purpose) Which job does this element mainly do for your book? Options: keeps track of something the reader must follow; marks a pattern that recurs; shapes how the story is presented; records planning the reader never sees.
3. [SCENARIO] (purpose) If this element were cut from your book entirely, what would a reader miss first?
4. [CHOICE] (layer) Where does this element live? Options: inside the story's world; in how the story is told; in the material around the story; only in your planning.
5. [OPEN] (key_details) Which detail of this element must read exactly the same every time it appears?
6. [SCALE] (key_details) How firmly must the details you have stored hold across the whole book? Scale: 1 = they may drift a little, 5 = they must never vary.
7. [OPEN] (connections) Which part of your book does this element touch most, and what does it do there?
8. [EITHER_OR] (connections) Does this element mostly serve one plotline, or does it run across several? Options: it serves one plotline; it runs across several.
9. [OPEN] (open_questions) What about this element have you still to decide?
10. [EITHER_OR] (open_questions) Is the most pressing open matter about what this element is, or about where it appears? Options: what it is; where it appears.
11. [SCALE] (prominence) How much of your book should this element be visible in? Scale: 1 = rarely seen on the page, 5 = present throughout.
12. [SCENARIO] (first_appearance) If a reader met this element for the first time in the scene you have stored as one of its connections, how much of it would they need to grasp straight away?
13. [EITHER_OR] (planned_changes) Between the opening and the end, does this element stay as it is or change on purpose? Options: it stays as it is; it changes on purpose.
14. [SCENARIO] (key_details, connections) If one of your stored details were contradicted in one of the parts of the book you listed, which of the two would you keep?
