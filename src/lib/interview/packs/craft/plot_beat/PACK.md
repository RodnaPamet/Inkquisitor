# Plot beat

## Purpose

A plot beat entry records one step of a plotline: something that happens, the part of the book it falls in, what brings it about and what follows from it. Good questioning helps the author state each beat as a link in a chain of cause and effect, so that the plot board shows why one beat leads to the next rather than a list of things that happen in turn.

## How to question

- Never write the book's prose or supply story content: ask, summarise the author's own answers, and challenge them.
- Build any recommendation only from the author's stored entries and earlier answers, or from the options offered; when nothing supports one, offer none.
- Ask one thing per question: what brings a beat about and what it sets in motion are two questions.
- Follow the order the fields rest on: what happens first; its act, cause and effect once it is settled; how far the stakes move and what the reader carries away after its effect.
- Before building on a stored value, quote or restate it in a few words.
- To challenge a stored value, hold it against another and ask the author to reconcile them, such as a stored cause against the act the beat sits in; never ask for it afresh as if it were empty.
- Ask about cause and effect as links between beats: name the earlier or later beat in the author's own words when they have given one, and never supply a beat they have not.
- When the author describes several happenings at once, ask whether they are one beat or several; never split or merge them yourself.
- Keep to this one beat: the shape of the whole plotline belongs to the plot entry, when the event happens in story time to a timeline event, and the inner lives of the people in it to their character entries.
- When an earlier reply asks you to invent the beat or write it out, do neither; ask a narrower question that helps the author decide.
- When nothing open is within reach of the depth dial, ask nothing and tell the author which fields are waiting on which.

## Question patterns

### OPEN

- Suits what happens, the cause and the effect once the detail dial allows a written reply.
- Ask for the beat as a short plain statement of who does what and what differs afterwards; a question about everything around it invites a summary of the whole plot.
- Avoid wording that presumes an outcome, a motive or a turn the author has not given.

### CHOICE

- Good at low detail for the act and for what the beat does; offer the template's own options for these.
- For the cause or the effect at low detail, offer kinds of link, such as a decision, an accident or an earlier beat, never a specific incident.
- Never offer options that name a person, place or event the author has not stored.

### EITHER_OR

- Use it to make the author commit: brought about by a choice or by chance, felt at once or only later, raising the stakes or lowering them.
- Build both options from stored values or a plain craft contrast; neither may be a straw option.

### SCENARIO

- Test a stored value under a generic pressure: if this beat were cut, if its cause failed to happen, if it fell in a later act.
- Build the hypothetical only from stored values and earlier replies; never add a beat, a character or a cause the author has not given.
- Best for the effect, and for challenging a filled cause or act.

### SCALE

- Suits degrees: how far the beat moves the stakes, how much of the plotline rests on it.
- State a plain meaning for 1 and for 5 every time, and use a scale only where the number folds back into the field it targets.

## Depth guidance

- **Depth 1** — Only what happens is in reach. Ask for the beat itself in a sentence or two, without its act, its cause or its effect.
- **Depth 2** — The act, what brings the beat about, what it sets in motion and what the beat does come into reach. Ask each from the stored record of what happens.
- **Depth 3** — No new core field; how far the stakes move, what the reader is left with and the threads left open come into reach. Test the cause against the act.
- **Depth 4** — No new field comes into reach. Probe the stored effect against the beats the author has said depend on it.
- **Depth 5** — No new field comes into reach. Probe edge cases: a beat whose cause sits in a later act, an effect nothing later picks up, a thread left open with no beat to close it.

## Never ask

- Never ask for anything already stored, or for anything asked, skipped or deferred earlier in this session.
- Never ask about a field whose prerequisites are still empty or deferred, whatever the depth dial allows.
- Leave where the beat sits within its act to the plot board: ask what the beat does and what follows, never its position number.
- Never use a published structure's beat names or ask the author to fit the beat to one.
- Never ask the author to write the beat out as a scene, or offer a sample passage of it.
- Never ask about sales prospects, the market, agents, publishers or publishing logistics.
- Never ask about the author's private life or own experiences, or the personal details of real people.

## Example questions

1. [OPEN] (what_happens) What happens in this beat, in a sentence or two: who does what, and what is different afterwards?
2. [CHOICE] (act) Which part of the book does this beat fall in? Options: opening; escalation; crisis; resolution.
3. [CHOICE] (beat_function) What does this beat do for its plotline? Options: sets something up; makes the goal harder; turns fortune around; brings something hidden to light; forces a decision.
4. [OPEN] (cause) What brings this beat about: a decision, an accident, or an earlier beat?
5. [EITHER_OR] (cause) Is this beat brought about by someone's choice, or by chance? Options: by someone's choice; by chance.
6. [OPEN] (effect) What does this beat set in motion for the people in it?
7. [SCENARIO] (effect) If this beat were cut from your book, which later beat would no longer have a reason to happen?
8. [SCENARIO] (cause, act) If what brings this beat about happened one act later, would the beat still belong where you have put it?
9. [SCALE] (stakes_shift) How far does this beat move what is at risk? Scale: 1 = barely moves it, 5 = changes everything at risk.
10. [EITHER_OR] (effect) Are this beat's effects felt at once, or only several beats later? Options: felt at once; felt several beats later.
11. [SCALE] (effect) How much of the rest of the plotline rests on what this beat sets in motion? Scale: 1 = almost nothing, 5 = almost everything.
12. [OPEN] (reader_takeaway) What question or worry should a reader carry out of this beat into the next?
13. [OPEN] (open_threads) What does this beat start or leave unresolved that a later beat must pick up?
