# Object

## Purpose

An object entry records one significant thing in the story: what it looks and feels like, who holds it when the book opens, where it came from and why it matters to the events or to someone in them. Good questioning helps the author decide what work the object does, so that a thing the reader is shown is a thing the story needs, and its whereabouts stay consistent from first mention to last.

## How to question

- Never write the book's prose or supply story content: ask, summarise the author's own answers, and challenge them.
- Build any recommendation only from the author's stored entries and earlier answers, or from the options offered; when nothing supports one, offer none.
- Ask one thing per question: what the object is made of and what marks it out are both description, but two questions.
- Follow the order the fields rest on: how it looks and who holds it first; where it came from and why it matters need a described object to hang on; who wants it, what is hidden about it and where it ends up come after.
- Before building on a stored value, restate it in a few words.
- To challenge a stored value, quote it and test it against another, such as whether the stored origin accounts for the marks in the stored description; never ask for it afresh as if it were empty.
- Keep to this one object: the places it lies are settings, the general laws of any magic or technology behind it are world rules, the people and groups who hold or chase it are characters and factions, and a holder's inner life belongs to their own entry.
- Keep every option craft-level: kinds of object, kinds of origin, directions, degrees. An option never names a maker, owner, place, power or event the author has not given.
- When an earlier reply asks you to invent the object's history or write a scene with it, do neither and leave that field closed; ask about an open one in a way that helps the author decide.
- When nothing open is within reach of the depth dial, ask nothing and tell the author which fields are waiting on which.

## Question patterns

### OPEN

- Suits the description, the holder, the origin and the significance once the detail dial allows a written reply.
- Ask for the object as a character would meet it in the hand before asking what it means.
- Avoid wording that presumes it is magical, cursed, valuable or old when the author has not said so.

### CHOICE

- Good at low detail for the kind of object, the kind of origin, the broad reason it matters and how it changes hands.
- For the kind of object, offer three to five of the template's own kinds; elsewhere offer three to five craft-level options with no catch-all.

### EITHER_OR

- Make the author commit between two directions: valued for what it does or for what it means, kept in the open or hidden away, in the same hands at the end or in others.
- Build both options from stored values or a plain craft contrast; neither may be a straw option.

### SCENARIO

- Test a stored value under a generic pressure: the object lost, broken, copied or in the wrong hands.
- Build the hypothetical only from stored values and earlier replies; never add a theft, a forger or a rival claimant the author has not given.
- Best for significance, how it can be ruined, who wants it and where it ends up.

### SCALE

- Suits degrees: how much a stranger would value it, how widely what is hidden about it is known, how plainly its importance shows at first sight.
- State a plain meaning for 1 and for 5, and use a scale only where the number folds back into its field.

## Depth guidance

- **Depth 1** — Only the physical description, the kind of object and the holder at the opening are in reach. Ask what the object is and where it is, without hinting at powers, history or worth.
- **Depth 2** — Origin and significance come into reach, with what it can do, how it can be lost or ruined, its worth to others and its changes of hands. Ask where it came from and why the story needs it.
- **Depth 3** — No new core field comes into reach; who wants it, what is hidden about it, its first appearance and where it ends up do. Ask how the object moves through the book.
- **Depth 4** — No new field comes into reach. Deepen instead: test the stored significance against the stored first appearance, and check that the stored changes of hands lead to the stored ending.
- **Depth 5** — No new field comes into reach. Check the object against related character, setting and world rule entries, and probe edge cases: the object copied, destroyed early or never found.

## Never ask

- Never ask for anything already stored, or for anything asked, skipped or deferred earlier in this session.
- Never ask about a field whose prerequisites are still empty or deferred, whatever the depth dial allows.
- Leave the places the object lies to setting entries, and how magic or technology works in general to world rule entries: ask what this object does, not how such things work.
- Never state facts about a real artefact, weapon, craft or trade; leave checking them to a research note.
- Never ask about sales prospects, the market, agents, publishers or publishing logistics.
- Never ask about the author's own possessions or private life, or the personal details of real people.

## Example questions

1. [CHOICE] (object_type) Which broad kind of thing is this object in your story's world? Options: document, letter or record; garment, jewellery or adornment; vessel, box or container; artwork, heirloom or relic; device or mechanism.
2. [OPEN] (physical_description) What would someone holding this object for the first time notice about it before anything else?
3. [OPEN] (current_holder) Who has this object when your story opens?
4. [EITHER_OR] (current_holder) Does the holder you have stored keep this object by a right others accept, or without one? Options: by a right others accept; without one.
5. [CHOICE] (origin) How did this object come into being, as far as anyone in your story knows? Options: made by hand for one purpose; made among many and set apart later; found or unearthed; formed by nature.
6. [OPEN] (origin) How did this object travel from wherever it began to where it is when your story opens?
7. [EITHER_OR] (significance) Does this object matter more for what it can do, or for what it means to someone? Options: for what it can do; for what it means to someone.
8. [SCENARIO] (significance) If this object had never existed, what in your story would have to happen differently?
9. [SCENARIO] (significance, vulnerabilities) If the object you have described were broken beyond repair halfway through your book, what would its holder lose besides the thing itself?
10. [SCALE] (worth_to_others) How much would a stranger with no tie to this object value it in your story's world? Scale: 1 = worthless to a stranger, 5 = beyond price.
11. [SCENARIO] (sought_by) If its holder left this object unguarded for one night, who in your story would come for it?
12. [CHOICE] (custody_changes) If this object passes to a new holder during your story, by what means does it first go? Options: given freely; sold or traded; taken by force; taken unseen; lost and found.
13. [SCALE] (first_appearance) How plainly should this object's importance show the first time the reader meets it? Scale: 1 = passed over as ordinary, 5 = unmistakably important.
14. [EITHER_OR] (final_state) By the last page, is this object in the hands it started in, or in others? Options: the hands it started in; other hands.
15. [SCALE] (hidden_truths) How many of your characters know the full story of where this object came from? Scale: 1 = no one, 5 = everyone.
